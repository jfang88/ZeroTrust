Speaker notes: Left is the NIST SP 800-207 core model. Every request from a subject goes through an enforcement point, which asks the decision point. The decision point weighs signals (identity strength, device health, threat intel, behaviour, data sensitivity) against policy and then opens or closes a session. Being "on the corporate network" earns nothing. Bottom band is the CISA ZTMM v2.0 view: five pillars that each mature at their own pace, held together by visibility, automation and governance. Key message: zero trust is an architecture and operating model, not a product. Most of the pieces are things we already buy; the change is making them feed one policy decision. That single decision point is also a new dependency, which is why resilience is designed in from day one (slide 5).

Speaker notes: These are the tests we will be held to. Discovery has a clear exit: inventory, evidence-based scores, a target design for the decision point, and a pilot shortlist. Pilots are judged on adoption, reduced standing privilege and user experience, and are kept small enough to ship in about 90 days. Two things are built in from the start rather than bolted on.
