Zero trust replaces network location with a per-request access decision, built across five pillars

Signals

Identity and MFA strength, device posture, threat intel, activity logs, data labels

Policy decision point

Policy engine decides each request; administrator opens or closes the session

Operating principles

  • Network location grants no trust
  • Least privilege, granted per session
  • Verify continuously, not once at login
  • Assume breach; limit blast radius

Subject

Users, devices, service accounts and workloads asking for access

Policy enforcement point

ZTNA or identity-aware proxy, API gateway, service mesh, host agent

Enterprise resources

Apps, APIs, data stores and cloud workloads, never directly reachable

Identity

Devices

Networks

Apps & workloads

Data

Cross-cutting: visibility & analytics · automation & orchestration · governance

Sources: NIST SP 800-207 (logical components); CISA Zero Trust Maturity Model v2.0 (pillars, cross-cutting capabilities)

1

Top half is the NIST SP 800-207 core model. Every request from a subject goes through an enforcement point, which asks the decision point. The decision point weighs signals — identity strength, device health, threat intel, behaviour, data sensitivity — against policy and then opens or closes a session. Being "on the corporate network" earns nothing. Bottom band is the CISA ZTMM v2.0 view: five pillars that each mature at their own pace, held together by visibility, automation and governance. Key message: zero trust is an architecture and operating model, not a product — most of the pieces are things we already buy; the change is making them feed one policy decision.
