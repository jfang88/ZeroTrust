How we will know it is working

DISCOVERY IS DONE WHEN

  • Crown-jewel data, apps and services are inventoried, with who and what can reach them
  • Each pillar is scored on the CISA scale, with evidence behind every score
  • A target design names which system makes the access decision
  • Two or three pilots are chosen, each with success measures

A PILOT SUCCEEDS WHEN

  • [__]% of the pilot group reach the resource only through the new decision point
  • Standing admin rights in scope fall to [__]
  • Sign-in friction and helpdesk tickets stay flat or fall
  • It ships in 90 days with one user group and one resource set

DESIGNED IN FROM DAY ONE

  • Break-glass access if the identity provider or decision point fails
  • A hardened identity provider, now the most valuable target
  • Privacy and cross-border review of access telemetry (PIPL, UK GDPR, HK PDPO)

Bracketed targets are set during discovery

5

These are the tests we will be held to. Discovery has a clear exit: inventory, evidence-based scores, a target design for the decision point, and a pilot shortlist. Pilots are judged on adoption, reduced standing privilege and user experience, and are kept small enough to ship in about 90 days. Two things are built in from the start rather than bolted on. First, zero trust makes the identity provider and policy engine a single point of dependency and a prime target, so we need break-glass access and hardening. Second, the telemetry that feeds access decisions is personal data and may cross borders between mainland China, Hong Kong and the UK, so privacy and legal review shape the architecture, not just the paperwork.
